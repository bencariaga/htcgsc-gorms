# Company Profile {#company-profile .HT-T1}

**Holy Trinity College of General Santos City**

## History {#history .H2-T2}

Holy Trinity College of General Santos City (HTCGSC) began as a small
family dream. In 1983, the Albano family started the Albano Piano Studio
in their own home. Seeing a need for more education, they planned for a
preschool, which officially opened in 1984 with only 28 students. This
small start was led by Dr. Consejo S. Albano and Madame Gertrudes T.
Albano.

As the years went by, the school grew very quickly. By 1986, it began
offering primary school grades and changed its name to Holy Trinity
School. Just two years later, it added a high school, and by 1989, it
was officially incorporated as a college, marking the birth of Holy
Trinity College. During this time, the college department opened with
programs like Criminology, Education, and Arts, starting with 124
students.

Throughout the 1990s and 2000s, HTCGSC continued to expand. It added
many new courses, including graduate programs and technical vocational
training. The school also built several new buildings to keep up with
its growing number of students. HTCGSC had reached the student
population marks of 5,000, 6,000, and 7,000 in school years 2012-2013,
2015-2016, and 2017-2018, respectively. Many programs run by HTCGSC had
received high accreditation levels.

Today, HTCGSC is known for its high standards and many achievements. It
has even sent teams to represent General Santos, SOCCKSARGEN, and the
Philippines, and to compete outside the educational institution, and won
in numerous competitions. From a tiny piano studio in a living room, it
has grown into a major educational institution, a wider campus, and
thousands of students. Its proud spirit is captured in the saying: "Once
a Trinitarian, always a Wildcat; and once a Wildcat, forever a
Trinitarian."

## Background and Operation of the Guidance and Testing Center {#background-and-operation-of-the-guidance-and-testing-center .H2-T2}

The Guidance and Testing Center of Holy Trinity College of General
Santos City is committed to providing a comprehensive and developmental
counseling program that addresses the academic, career, and
personal-social needs of students. Guided by the institution's core
values, the Center seeks to promote the holistic growth of learners by
supporting their intellectual, psycho-emotional, social, and physical
well-being. Its programs are carefully designed to assist students in
gaining a deeper understanding of themselves, developing their
potential, and achieving their personal and academic goals while
contributing to the overall formation of the academic community.

In keeping with the demands of the times, the Center has adopted
innovative approaches to ensure that its services remain accessible and
relevant. Alongside traditional in-person counseling, testing, and
symposia, it has integrated digital platforms such as Google Meet, Zoom,
Google Forms, and social media channels to accommodate the evolving
needs of students and stakeholders. Through these efforts, the Guidance
and Testing Center continues to serve as a reliable partner in nurturing
student success, resilience, and holistic development.

## Vision and Mission of the Guidance and Testing Center {#vision-and-mission-of-the-guidance-and-testing-center .H2-T2}

their potential, fostering healthy relationships, and preparing them to
become well-rounded individuals ready to meet the challenges of life."

# Program Objectives {#program-objectives .HT-T1}

The primary goal of this On-the-Job Training (OJT) is to place the
learned IT concepts and skills into practice in a professional setting.
It is designed to help a student transition from academic learning to
professional practice by exposing them to the actual daily operations of
the IT workplace. The trainee gets hands-on experience to build and
maintain a real system. The program intends to build confidence and
adaptability. By working alongside professional, the student would be
able to learn how to solve practical problems, manage projects, and
communicate effectively within the workplace. Whether it is about
developing applications, managing databases, or handling office
routines, the objectives ensure that the trainee is equipped with enough
technical competence and skills needed to succeed in a competitive IT
job market. The following are the skills and knowledge the trainee
acquires during the training program:

A.  Office Routine and Workflow Familiarization

B.  Collaboration with Guidance and Testing Center Staff

C.  User Interface Design and Implementation using Tailwind CSS and
    Alpine.js

D.  Web Application Development using Laravel Livewire

E.  Database Management using MySQL in Local and PostgreSQL in
    Production

F.  Establishment and integration of OTP-based Authentication, and
    Email-based and SMS-based Communication using Simple Mail Transfer
    Protocol (SMTP) and TextBee SMS API

G.  Establishment and integration of GTC's Google Form named "HTC
    Guidance Referral / Appointment Form" to the system while using
    Google Apps Script

H.  Real-Time System Functionality and Testing

I.  Deployment of the System to Render

J.  Transfer of the Full Ownership and Maintenance of the System to
    Another Person or to Other People via GitHub

# Final Written Report {#final-written-report .HT-T1}

## Scope of Work {#scope-of-work .H2-T4}

The scope of work for my on-the-job training (OJT) is to develop the
**Holy Trinity College of General Santos City -- Guidance Office Records
Management System** (**HTCGSC-GORMS**), a full-stack web application
made for **HTCGSC -- Guidance and Testing Center** (**HTCGSC-GTC**). It
makes use of the **TALL Stack:** **T**ailwind CSS, **A**lpine.js,
**L**aravel, and **L**ivewire. The system development involves the
**graphic user interface** (**GUI**) **design**, **server** using
**Apache**, **database management** using **MySQL** and **phpMyAdmin**,
**application program interface** (**API**) **integrations**, **system
testing**, and eventual **cloud deployment**. These integrations are
about establishing communication channels via **Gmail's Simple Mail
Transfer Protocol** (**SMTP**) for **emails**, and **TextBee's API** for
**text messages**, as well as establishing a bridge for the **online
data collection** from **Google Forms** to the system using **Google
Apps Script** and **Ngrok**. This development also includes the
deployment of the system to the cloud as in production-level operation
using **Render** (web hosting), **Docker** (virtualization platform),
**PostgreSQL** and **TablePlus** (database management), and **Nginx**
(server).

## Key Features {#key-features .H2-T4}

**Security** -- The system uses primarily email addresses and optionally
phone numbers, with user passwords to make users log in. One-time
passwords (OTP) are also in the system on top this feature in order to
verify if the email addresses and phone numbers used for the login came
from legitimate users, the GTC employees. The system also allows users
to change their passwords, email addresses, and phone numbers, where the
first one logs out the users doing this action, and the last two
redirect these users to the OTP page and makes the system send emails
and text messages depending on what type of change these users requested
to do so.

**Dashboard** -- This serves as the landing page of the system after
every successful authentication. This page displays three (6)
easy-to-understand metrics, specifically "Total Students" (currently
referring as the total number of students who have indirectly used the
system, because they only used Google Forms to connect with the system),
"Total Form Submissions" (overall submissions accepted as appointments),
"Today's Appointments" (appointments for the current day), "Total
Referrals" (overall students being referred), "Total Self-Referrers"
(overall students referring to themselves), and "Total
Non-Self-Referrers" (overall students referring to other students), with
line charts for "Form Submissions" and "Students Registered", with each
of them measured every one day from 30 days ago to 16 days ago and from
15 days ago to the current day.

**Google Forms data connection, collection, and sanitization** -- By
means of cloud-based platforms, such as Google Apps Script and Ngrok /
Render, the system is able to get and collect the data from the form
submissions of the students. The system also manipulates these data at a
minimum to make all these data acceptable by the system without any
errors or empty data fields when there are some mismatches between its
database's data fields their Google Forms counterparts, while the
sanitized data are still faithful to the original, since Google Forms do
not offer these guard rails the same as the system, when the data comes
from the outside of the system.

**Intuitiveness of Web Pages with Tables** -- The system web pages for
users, students, appointments, and audit logs have each a dynamic table
that would scale up when the system is used regularly for a significant
amount of time. In these pages, there are user interface components to
adjust the size of the page, to search, to sort, to filter, and to
paginate, with easy-to-understand column names, button names, and data
categories.

**Submissions** -- The system reads log files that contain raw data
submitted by students via the form in Google Forms named "HTC Guidance
Referral / Appointment Form".

**QR Code Page** -- This web page in the system is dedicated for not
only the Quick Response (QR) code for the specific form in Google Forms,
but also the Uniform Resource Locators (URL) for the form's view and
edit pages.

**Reports** -- The users can name and create reports in the system, and
download them as report files in spreadsheet (.xlsx) and PDF formats,
containing valuable pieces of information with date range when these
have existed ever since.

**Audit Logs** -- The system reads log files that contain data for the
chronological events that happened in the system done by the users.

**Appointment Reminder Automation** -- With help of the "cron-job", the
system would automatically send email or SMS notifications to students
for their GTC appointments coming on schedules one day or shorter
lengths of time from the current time.

## Results and Accomplishment {#results-and-accomplishment .H2-T4}

During the OJT period, the development of the HTCGSC-GORMS has given
significant technical accomplishments. Early in the project, successful
connections were established for real-time communication using Gmail
SMTP for emails and the TextBee API for SMS. In order to handle student
referrals and appointments to be requested online, the \"HTC Guidance
Referral / Appointment Form\" was created using Google Forms and
successfully connected to the system using Ngrok and Google Apps Script.

As the system grew, the user interface was continuously improved for
better user experience. For example, I removed the standalone
\"Referrals\" page and merged the page to the \"Appointments\" page. The
page for the QR code has a download function integrated to make
accessing the forms easier. After several weeks, much of the backend
logic has been successfully implemented, with adding an appointment
rescheduling feature, strictly excluding weekends and holidays from the
booking calendar, and making data filtering functional across multiple
pages.

To make the first version of the system complete, I added the
"Submissions", "Reports", and "Audit Logs" pages. After putting all the
necessary pages together, I implemented the SOLID Principles for the
backend and Atomic Design for the frontend to make writing code more
efficient and for the code files themselves to be long enough to be
readable and modular to connect with each other. After this, I set up a
repository on GitHub for HTCGSC-GORMS system project folders and files.
I also connected the repository to Render as my web hosting service. To
make this possible, I replaced each of the following: MySQL for
PostgreSQL, phpMyAdmin for TablePlus, Apache for Nginx, and Ngrok for
Render. I also added a scheduler API, called the "cron-job" to handle
the automation to send GTC appointment notifications to students.

## Problems Encountered {#problems-encountered .H2-T4}

Along the way of the development of the system, several technical
challenges tested my skills in problem-solving. The first one was
figuring out how to connect the local development server to the internet
to receive live data from Google Forms. This required extensive research
and testing of multiple tunneling services gathered in this research
before finally settling to Ngrok as the most reliable solution. The
second one happened when I attempted to make a data filtering component
functional in the pages for users, students, appointments, and audit
logs. The first attempt to implement this complex logic was not
successful. However, with persistent debugging and code review, I
successfully made the data filtering functional on the second attempt.
The third one is implementing SOLID Principles and Atomic Design.
Although they are some of the default best practices to implement in a
system or application, to make them work in HTCGSC-GORMS is not easily
achievable. With persistent learning about these concepts on the
Internet, I successfully implement these best practices in order to make
the codebase of the system more manageable to read and modify. And the
last one is unit testing, where it was easier for me to test on the
browser than on the Laravel's packages for the unit testing, although
this is a best practice to make the system look more professional for
future programmers and developers to manage this system as a GitHub
repository.

**TALL Stack:** **T**ailwind CSS, **A**lpine.js, **L**aravel, and

using **XAMPP**. I prioritized security and communication early on by
implementing **OTP-based authentication**. I also successfully connected

system to send automated email and text notifications to users. A major
focus of the project was creating a seamless way for students to submit
requests. I created the "**HTC Guidance Referral / Appointment Form**"
using **Google Forms**. To link these online form submissions to my
local database, I researched and tested several tunneling services,
ultimately choosing **Ngrok**. Using **Google Apps Script** and

real-time data bridging. To ensure the system remained secure, I also
wrote specific PHP classes to sanitize the incoming data from these
forms. As development progressed, I continuously refined the user
interface to ensure it was clean and user-friendly. I made a decision to
remove the "**Referrals**" page and merge its functionalities into the
"**Appointments**" page to simplify the workflow. I also a generated
downloadable QR code for students to have access to the form. I tackled
advanced backend features. After an initial failed attempt, I
successfully implemented functional data filtering across multiple
pages. I also added features to reschedule appointments and then
programmed the calendar to strictly exclude weekends and holidays. Aside
from programming, I also given assistance for the workplace by
successfully troubleshooting an IP address conflict on the computer next
to me. In the later stages of system development, I added even more
professional features. I wrote the "**Reports**" page where users can

next web developers and programmers. In conclusion, my internship was an
